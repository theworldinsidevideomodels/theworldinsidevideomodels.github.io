The World Inside Video Models

What world knowledge emerges from video pretraining, how can we evaluate it, and how can it enable reasoning, planning, and action?

Call for papers Important dates
Format One-day, in-person workshop with invited talks, oral presentations, posters, and a panel discussion.
Location ICLR 2027 in California. Venue to be announced.
Audience Researchers in video generation, world modeling, representation learning, task learning, robotics, planning, and evaluation.

Scope

Video models are rapidly moving from plausible clip generators toward systems that encode structured knowledge about objects, dynamics, physical interactions, actions, and environments. Recent work suggests that during pretraining such models can acquire reusable knowledge from visual experience that can be used for rule learning, planning, few-shot repurposing of video models, and marks video pretraining as a route to more data-efficient visual intelligence compared to language models. This workshop aims to bring together researchers in video generation, world modeling, representation learning, task learning, robotics, planning, and evaluation studying how large-scale video models acquire reusable representations of the real world and whether such knowledge can support downstream visual tasks in a way analogous to how language models are used beyond text generation.

More specifically, this workshop focuses on:

What Video Models Learn

Dynamics, affordances, physical constraints, task structure, visual intelligence, and the limits of learned world knowledge.

Probing and Interpretation

Probes, benchmarks, counterfactual tests, interventions, and failure analyses that separate reusable structure from surface correlations.

Using World Knowledge

Interactive generation, planning, visual reasoning, robot learning, task adaptation, and decision making from observation.

Training Better Models

Objectives, architectures, adaptation methods, memory mechanisms, data curricula, and evaluation loops for downstream utility.

Generalization

Generalization across domains, environments, tasks, interaction conditions, action spaces, and long temporal horizons.

Datasets and Results

Datasets, demos, benchmarks, early-stage work, position papers, and negative results for video models and world knowledge.

Key Questions

01

Emergence

What forms of world knowledge do video models acquire?

Objects, agents, actions, dynamics, affordances, physical constraints, and temporal structure.

02

Evaluation

How can world knowledge be identified and tested?

Probes, benchmarks, counterfactual tests, and failure analyses for reusable world knowledge.

03

Transfer

How can world knowledge support downstream tasks?

Interactive generation, planning, visual reasoning, robot learning, task adaptation, and decision making.

04

Training

How can training enable richer world knowledge to emerge?

Objectives, architectures, adaptation, memory, curricula, and evaluation loops.

Submissions

Format

4-page extended abstracts excluding references and appendicies, submitted through OpenReview. Accepted work will be non-archival.

Contribution Types

Original research papers related to studying the representation of the real world in video models.

Review Process

Each submission will receive three reviews whenever possible, with conflicts handled through OpenReview and organizer checks.

The workshop is intended for fresh community-building discussion, not for work already published in major machine-learning or adjacent venues.
